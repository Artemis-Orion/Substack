# So Long and Thanks for All the Oil

An indictment of rampant consumerism, A Perfect Circle's music video "So
Long, and Thanks for All the Fish" elegantly captures how society
distracts itself from the chaos and destruction caused by modern
lifestyles through consumerist hedonism. Throughout the video, many
points from Wilson et al's "On Petrocultures" are neatly reflected. An
absurdist romp that highlights society's unwillingness to give up its
consumerist culture in the face of death, destruction, and absolute
chaos, "So Long, and Thanks for All the Fish" cuts deep with its
scathing commentary, outright mocking the United States' cultural
nostalgia for the 1950s and the deeply ingrained culture of consumerism
that era ultimately led to.

The video opens on a 1950s style scene with a television broadcast on
the destructive power of a nuclear explosion playing on a television
set. The broadcast cuts to a commercial for a made-up tuna brand called
Flying with the Fishes. This leads into a surprisingly upbeat song with
contrasting lyrics overlaid on a rather dark and gruesome video that
starts off innocently enough in a small grocery store. Soon enough the
audience is faced with a reality that is quite literally going up in
flames around the housewife protagonist whom the camera follows through
most of the video. Throughout the video the protagonist seems to be in
her own little world that allows her to ignore all the turmoil around
her. Or rather, the world itself seems to be in denial of the truth of
the reality that surrounds them, seemingly distracting themselves
through consumerist and hedonistic practices. A Perfect Circle's video
condemns consumerism as a cultural practice, noting specifically the
lyric, "We wasted every second Dime/ On diets, lawyers, shrinks and apps
and flags and plastic surgery," and the later lyric, "We wasted every
second dime/ On politicians, fancy water and guns and plastic surgery,"
which highlight society's tendency to spend money on distractions and
status symbols.

The decision to set "So Long, and Thanks for All the Fish" in the midst
of the Atomic Age reflects an idea from "On Petrocultures" that "The
logics that shape our present have been passed down to us, some dating
back millennia and grounded in religions and cultural beliefs about what
it means to be human and to live in society" (Wilson et al. 9). These
logics make it difficult to address environmental problems because they
are almost inextricably entangled with society's perception of identity
and culture. To address these issues society would need to fundamentally
change the very fabric of its being---a Herculean task to say the least.

Another idea expressed in "So Long, and Thanks for All the Fish" is the
fantasy world in which everyone seems to be living. Everyone ignores the
burning hellscapes and death surrounding them, continuing their
existence as if nothing is happening all with a friendly smile---that,
at times, appears more manic---and a wave. The result is a rather fluid
journey through this snapshot of their lives which illustrates rather
nicely "the ideology of 'lubricity'---the contemporary fantasy world we
inhabit, one of smooth flows of people, resources, and capital that
demands intensified use of petrocarbons to keep everything moving"
(Wilson et al. 9). The people almost seem to say with their expressions,
"This is fine. Everything is fine," while their world incinerates around
them.

The very core of Western culture is tied to almost mythical ideals that
are heavily dependent on fossil fuels or as Wilson et al. put it,
"Freedom, identity, success: our deepest ideals and most prominent
social fantasies are mediated and enabled by the energies of fossil
fuels" (11). The first time "So Long, and Thanks for All the Fish"
confronts the audience with its anti-consumerism line, "We wasted every
second Dime/ On diets, lawyers, shrinks and apps and flags and plastic
surgery," (A Perfect Circle 0:49-0:59) the imagery displayed is that of
dolphins flying through the air that transition to bomber planes at the
start of the next line. This imagery gives the audience its first
glimpse of the fantasy world the people see instead of their reality.
The next time the audience is confronted, it is with a slight variation,
"We wasted every second dime/ On politicians, fancy water and guns and
plastic surgery," (A Perfect Circle 2:25-2:35) over a scene of a rather
hefty man delightedly sitting down to a feast that cuts to a
woman---presumably his wife---destroying a piece of abstract modern art.
All of which mocks Western society's overindulgence and tendency to view
everything as disposable. The last time the audience is confronted, the
song reprises the initial lyric, "We wasted every second Dime/ On diets,
lawyers, shrinks and apps and flags and plastic surgery" (A Perfect
Circle 4:08-4:18) the world is diving into total chaos as the people
wear almost manic grins, signifying how society's continued denial leads
to chaos and destruction.

The video ends with an atomic explosion killing everyone within the
setting; and, with all the atomic and nuclear references scattered
throughout the song and video, it is likely not just a metaphor for the
impending catastrophic fate of mankind but also a reference to the
beginnings of the Anthropocene epoch itself. With that in mind, the
scathing commentary and sarcastic nature of "So Long, and Thanks for All
the Fish" takes on a whole new meaning, wherein consumerism is the major
driving force of destruction in the Anthropocene. Consumerism demands
the continued use of oil and its byproducts, sparks wars to obtain more
oil to sustain its mechanisms, and effects climate change the likes of
which the Earth has never seen by burning fossil fuels at unsustainable
levels.
